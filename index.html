Question 1 — auto 1fr Correct choice of grid tracks You answered that you would use: grid-template-rows: auto 1fr; That's correct for the behavior I described. The first row is sized according to its content and the second row receives the remaining available space, subject to its content-size constraints. However, you also said you would set the navigation's height to 100%. You generally don't need to do that. A Grid item normally stretches to fill its assigned grid area unless you change its alignment or sizing behavior. The important distinction is: - grid-template-rows defines the sizes of the tracks. - height controls the size of an element. - align-self controls how a particular grid item aligns inside its allocated area. These are related, but they are not interchangeable. Question 2 — Content should determine page height Correct overall principle You said that the main content should determine the height of the page. That's generally the right approach for a content-heavy application like Book Notes. However, you also suggested that min-height should specifically be placed on main. That's not necessarily the best choice. Understand the difference between two requirements Requirement A: The page should fill the viewport even when there's little content. This is often a responsibility of the outer page layout, such as body or a page-shell container. Requirement B: The page should grow when its content becomes taller. This is normally accomplished by allowing the content to remain in normal document flow without imposing unnecessary fixed heights. For Book Notes, imagine a library containing just one book versus a library containing 100 books. The page should look sensible with one book and be able to grow naturally with 100 books. You don't necessarily need to assign a minimum height to main to achieve this.
